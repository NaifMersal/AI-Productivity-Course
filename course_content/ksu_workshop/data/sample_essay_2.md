Title: Remote Learning and Student Engagement in Saudi Universities

Remote learning became very common in Saudi universities and it is still used today in many courses. Students attend lectures online, submit assignments through learning platforms, and sometimes never meet their instructors in person. This essay talks about remote learning and student engagement and why it is an important topic for universities to think about.

Many people say that remote learning is bad for engagement. Al-Dossari (2023) did a study and found that students in online courses were less engaged than students in face-to-face courses. The study looked at attendance and participation. Most students said they felt isolated and found it hard to focus during long online lectures. This shows that remote learning can reduce engagement in some cases.

On the other hand, remote learning has some benefits too. It is flexible and students can watch recorded lectures at any time, which is good for students who work or live far from campus. Some tools like discussion boards and quizzes can help students stay involved. Al-Mutairi (2024) mentioned that interactive online activities increased participation, although the study was small and only covered one department, so the results may not apply everywhere.

In conclusion, remote learning has both advantages and disadvantages for student engagement. Universities should think carefully about how they design online courses. More research is needed to fully understand this topic and to find the best way to keep students engaged in remote learning environments.
