Title: The Impact of Social Media on Academic Performance

Social media has become an integral part of daily life for university students. Platforms such as Twitter, Instagram, and TikTok are used for communication, entertainment, and sometimes even learning. However, there is growing concern about the negative effects of social media on academic performance. This essay examines the relationship between social media usage and student grades, drawing on recent studies conducted in Saudi Arabian universities.

According to Al-Harbi (2023), students who spend more than three hours per day on social media platforms have grade point averages that are 0.4 points lower than students who limit their usage to less than one hour. The study surveyed 400 students at three Saudi universities and controlled for factors such as major, year of study, and prior academic achievement. The findings suggest a moderate negative correlation between social media use and GPA.

However, the relationship is not straightforward. Al-Jaber (2024) found that students who used social media specifically for academic purposes — joining study groups, following educational accounts, and sharing resources — actually performed better than those who did not use social media at all. The key variable appears to be whether usage is active and purposeful versus passive and recreational. This distinction is important for educators who may be considering blanket bans on social media in educational settings.

Time management plays a crucial role in mediating this relationship. Al-Qahtani (2023) demonstrated that students who reported strong time management skills were able to use social media for up to two hours daily without any negative impact on their grades. In contrast, students with poor time management showed academic decline even with less than one hour of daily social media use. This suggests that interventions should focus on time management training rather than simply restricting access.

In conclusion, the impact of social media on academic performance depends largely on how it is used and the individual characteristics of the student. Rather than viewing social media as inherently harmful, educators and policymakers should promote purposeful use and help students develop the time management skills necessary to balance their online and academic lives.
